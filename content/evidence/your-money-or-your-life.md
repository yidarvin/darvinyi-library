# Your Money or Your Life — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These first-party sources support the chapter's original account of the
book's relationship between money, work time, deliberate spending, and financial
independence. The chapter is a synthesis in new prose and original diagrams, not a
reproduction of the book's text, worksheets, or figures.

- Vicki Robin and Joe Dominguez, [*Your Money or Your Life*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/303637/your-money-or-your-life-by-vicki-robin-and-joe-dominguez-foreword-by-mr-money-mustache/), Penguin Random House. The publisher describes the revised edition's nine-step program, its focus on mindful saving rather than strict budgeting, and its updated coverage of index funds, freelance income, and digital financial tracking. It also supplies the chapter's outbound purchase link.
- Vicki Robin, [*Your Money Or Your Life* summary](https://vickirobin.com/your-money-or-your-life-summary/). The authors' official site explains financial intelligence, integrity, and independence; defines money as a trade of life energy; outlines an adjusted hourly-wage calculation; and describes tracking, monthly review, and a capital-and-expenses crossover. This is the direct source for the chapter's conceptual model, while all explanations and visuals here are new.
- Vicki Robin, [book page](https://vickirobin.com/your-money-or-your-life/). Robin's official description frames the book as tools for taking control of money in order to make a life, and identifies debt reduction, savings, reordered material priorities, and values alignment as program aims.
- Vicki Robin, [author biography](https://vickirobin.com/about/). Robin explains the book's origins in questioning dependence on money and consumption, and describes Dominguez's work on spending less, earning purposefully, saving, and leaving work that depletes life. This supports the chapter's account of the book as a values-and-time framework, not merely an investing guide.

The chapter's caveat is deliberate. The official publisher says the revised edition modernized
the investing material, so the original program's historical interest-rate examples are not
treated as current financial advice. The chapter also distinguishes a reflective personal
finance framework from a claim that every household has equal capacity to save or choose work.
