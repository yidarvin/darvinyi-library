# Bargaining for Advantage — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These publisher and Wharton sources support the chapter's account of
G. Richard Shell's preparation-centered approach to negotiation. The chapter is an original
synthesis. Its prose and diagrams do not reproduce the book's text, figures, or cover.

- Penguin Random House, [*Bargaining for Advantage*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/298835/bargaining-for-advantage-by-g-richard-shell/), publisher page. The current edition is presented as a systematic, step-by-step method for negotiating effectively in a way that fits the negotiator, including a diagnostic for individual strengths and weaknesses and guidance on low-power and impasse situations.
- Wharton Executive Education, [*Bargaining for Advantage: Negotiation Workshop*](https://executiveeducation.wharton.upenn.edu/for-individuals/all-programs/bargaining-for-advantage-negotiation-workshop/), official program page. The program's topical outline names styles, goals, relationships, standards, interests, leverage, value creation, and multiparty bargaining as parts of Shell's approach.
- Knowledge at Wharton, ["Your Move: Book Offers Negotiation Strategies for Global Executives"](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/your-move-book-offers-negotiation-strategies-for-global-executives/), 2005. In an interview about the book, Shell describes writing for executives who had not had time to think systematically about how negotiation works and the psychological difficulties it creates.
- Penguin Random House, [G. Richard Shell](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/authors/57830/g-richard-shell/), author page. The publisher identifies Shell as a University of Pennsylvania Wharton management professor and lists *Bargaining for Advantage* among his books.

### Scope note

Preparation can clarify a negotiator's choices, but it does not erase unequal power, create
informed consent, or make an unsafe counterpart safe. The chapter therefore treats a credible
alternative as one option among others, alongside organizational safeguards, formal processes,
and outside support when they are needed.
