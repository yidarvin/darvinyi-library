# Indistractable — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These author and publisher sources support the chapter's account of
the book's attention framework, timeboxing, external triggers, and precommitments. The
chapter is an original synthesis of ideas, not a reproduction of the book's text or
figures.

- Nir Eyal, [The 4 Steps to Becoming Indistractable](https://www.nirandfar.com/how-to-be-indistractable/). The author's guide defines internal and external triggers, distinguishes intentional action from distraction, and presents the four moves that inform this chapter: address internal triggers, reserve time for intended activity, handle external prompts, and use precommitment.
- Nir Eyal, [The Skill of the Future is Being ‘Indistractable’](https://www.nirandfar.com/skill-of-the-future/). This author article explains the book's view that both internal and external prompts can lead to either intended or unintended action. It supports the chapter's distinction between an activity and the plan it displaces, while the chapter's comparison diagram is an original alternative visual.
- Nir Eyal, [Timeboxing: Why It Works and How to Get Started](https://www.nirandfar.com/timeboxing/). The author describes timeboxing as planning what to do and when to do it, then refining the plan iteratively. It grounds the chapter's calendar practice and its treatment of a block as a revisable commitment rather than a rigid forecast.
- Nir Eyal, [Unlocking Focus with the Distraction Tracker](https://www.nirandfar.com/distraction-tracker/). Eyal's tracker advises identifying the discomfort that preceded a distraction, recording it, and exploring it with curiosity. It supports the chapter's short observational exercise and its rejection of self-contempt as a useful response.
- Nir Eyal, [Can Someone with ADHD be ‘Indistractable’?](https://www.nirandfar.com/adhd-but-indistractable/). The author notes that values and timeboxing can require individual adaptation for people with ADHD. It informs the page's scope note, which deliberately does not present the framework as diagnosis or treatment.
- BenBella Books, [*Indistractable: How to Control Your Attention and Choose Your Life*](https://www.benbellabooks.com/shop/indistractable/). Publisher record and the chapter's outbound reader link for the 2019 book by Nir Eyal with Julie Li.

### Scope note

The sources document Eyal's framework and practices. They do not establish that a single
attention method will work across workplaces, homes, health conditions, or access needs.
The chapter therefore presents the practices as adjustable experiments and explicitly
separates personal attention tactics from structural problems and clinical care.
