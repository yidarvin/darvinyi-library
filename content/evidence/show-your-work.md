# Show Your Work! — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. This chapter is an original synthesis of Kleon's ideas. Its diagrams are
new conceptual explanations, not reproductions of the book's illustrations or layout.

- Austin Kleon, [*Show Your Work!*](https://austinkleon.com/show-your-work/). Kleon's official
  page identifies the book as a guide for people uncomfortable with self-promotion and outlines
  its concerns with ongoing process, sharing small pieces, telling stories, teaching, avoiding
  spam, receiving criticism, selling, and persistence. It informed the chapter's emphasis on
  selective process-sharing, practical generosity, and a sustained public practice.
- Hachette Book Group, [*Show Your Work!*](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/austin-kleon/9780761178972/9780761178972/).
  The publisher records Workman Publishing Company, the March 6, 2014 publication date, and
  the paperback ISBN used for the outbound reader link. Its description frames the book around
  discoverability, sharing process, credit, and the risks of oversharing.
- Austin Kleon, ["Show Your Work! Tour Notebook"](https://austinkleon.com/page/384/).
  Kleon describes his notebooks as private thinking spaces rather than material to make public.
  This supports the chapter's caveat that an open practice needs boundaries and does not require
  exposing raw private work.

### Scope note

The chapter treats visibility as a communication practice, not a causal guarantee of discovery
or income. Platform incentives, existing networks, time, safety, rights, collaboration
agreements, and unequal access can all affect who is able to share and who is seen. The practice
cards therefore prioritize a sustainable archive, consent, attribution, and useful explanation
over audience growth.
