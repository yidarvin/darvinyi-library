# The Tao of Pooh — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. This chapter treats Hoff's book as a popular, literary introduction to
Taoist themes. The publisher page was used to verify the book's framing; the primary texts were
consulted separately so the page does not mistake a modern character analogy for the full
tradition. The prose is original synthesis, and every diagram is an original teaching device.

- Benjamin Hoff, [*The Tao of Pooh*](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Tao-of-Pooh/Benjamin-Hoff/9781668259641). The official publisher description identifies the 1982 book as an introduction to Taoist philosophy through Winnie-the-Pooh and foregrounds simplicity, naturalness, humility, and *wu wei*. It supports the page's description of Hoff's framing and supplies its outbound reader link. No passages or illustrations from the book are reproduced.
- Laozi, [*Dao De Jing*](https://ctext.org/dao-de-jing). The Chinese Text Project's received text was consulted for recurring treatment of non-forcing, restraint, yielding, and sufficiency. It grounds the page's account of *wu wei* and its distinction between proportionate action and passivity; the chapter reorganizes these ideas rather than following the text's order.
- Zhuangzi, [“Nourishing the Lord of Life”](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/nourishing-the-lord-of-life/ens). This primary-text chapter contains the story of Cook Ding, where practiced attention follows the openings in what it meets. It informs the page's careful account of skilled action as trained responsiveness, not as magic or idleness.
- Zhuangzi, [*Zhuangzi*](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/ens). The received text was consulted for its use of stories, shifting perspectives, and natural responsiveness. It supports the evidence note's caution that a compact modern personality allegory cannot stand in for a varied early Chinese corpus.

### Attribution boundary

The page's concentric circles, flow, spectrum, comparison, and Venn diagrams are original
library illustrations. Neither Hoff's book nor the classical texts supply these figures. “Plain
attention,” “enough for the purpose,” and “fitting action” are this library's practical labels,
not named models attributed to Hoff, Laozi, or Zhuangzi. The page does not reproduce Milne's
characters, dialogue, or E. H. Shepard's artwork.
