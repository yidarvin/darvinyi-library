# Attached — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These publisher, author-affiliated, and original research sources inform
this chapter's account of *Attached*. The chapter is an original synthesis. Its diagrams are
original conceptual SVGs, not copies of the book's prose, illustrations, or cover.

- Pan Macmillan, [*Attached*](https://www.panmacmillan.com/authors/amir-levine/attached/9781447205425), Amir Levine and Rachel Heller, 2011 UK edition. The publisher describes the book's secure, anxious, and avoidant framing and provides the reader link used on this page.
- Cindy Hazan and Phillip R. Shaver, ["Romantic Love Conceptualized as an Attachment Process"](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/Psyc591Readings/HazanShaver1987.pdf), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1987. The original adult romantic attachment article translated infant attachment patterns into a romantic relationship context and used secure, avoidant, and anxious-ambivalent categories as its initial framework.
- R. Chris Fraley and Phillip R. Shaver, ["Adult Romantic Attachment: Theoretical Developments, Emerging Controversies, and Unanswered Questions"](https://adultattachment.faculty.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/66/2015/09/Fraley_2000_Adult-romantic-attachment.pdf), *Review of General Psychology*, 2000. This review traces the adult theory's development, treats a partner's accessibility and responsiveness as relevant to felt security, and makes clear that several early claims required revision or further evidence.
- Phillip R. Shaver and R. Chris Fraley, [Self-Report Measures of Adult Attachment](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~rcfraley/measures/measures.html), University of California, Davis and University of Illinois. Their research guide explains the shift from the original three descriptions to continuous anxiety and avoidance measures, including the Experiences in Close Relationships scales.
- R. Chris Fraley, Amanda M. Vicary, Claudia C. Brumbaugh, Glenn I. Roisman, and others, ["Patterns of Stability in Adult Attachment: An Empirical Test of Two Models of Continuity and Change"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21707199/), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2011. Across a 30-day daily study and a one-year weekly study, the authors found evidence consistent with an underlying stable factor alongside temporary variation. It supports the chapter's caution that stability does not mean immutability.

### Scope note

Attachment research describes patterns in expectations and regulation within close relationships.
It does not diagnose a person, determine who is safe to date, establish that childhood alone caused
an adult pattern, or make a harmful relationship workable. The page's exercises are communication
prompts for relationships in which direct conversation is safe. They are not a substitute for
trauma-informed care, domestic-violence resources, or professional mental-health support.
