# Cosmos — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. These primary institutional sources inform the chapter's scientific
claims and its account of the book's public purpose. The chapter is original synthesis,
not a reproduction of the book or its images.

- Library of Congress, [Cosmos: An appreciation](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mss85590.050/?sp=1&st=text). Sagan's 1980 manuscript describes the television project as an experiment in communicating science and addresses the gap between an age shaped by science and public understanding of it.
- National Aeronautics and Space Administration, [Overview: The Universe](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/overview/). NASA's current account places the universe at about 13.8 billion years old and explains that the cosmic microwave background is light from an early, once-opaque universe that became transparent roughly 380,000 years after the beginning.

  Figure 82.1 uses that perspective only to distinguish nearby, million-year, and billion-year lookback views. It deliberately gives the far galaxy no present-day distance, because a cosmological lookback time is not a one-to-one present-distance conversion while space expands.
- National Aeronautics and Space Administration, [Massive Stars: Engines of Creation](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/massive-stars-engines-of-creation-infographic/). The agency explains that massive stars fuse progressively heavier elements and distribute them when they explode, supplying material for later generations of stars, planets, and life.
- National Aeronautics and Space Administration, [Voyager 1's Pale Blue Dot](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1s-pale-blue-dot/). The mission record documents Voyager 1's 1990 image of Earth from 3.7 billion miles from the Sun and Sagan's role in proposing the solar-system portrait.
- National Aeronautics and Space Administration, [Solar System, Galaxy, Universe: What's the Difference?](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/night-sky-network/solar-system-galaxy-universe-whats-the-difference/). This overview supports the page's nested-scale diagram: Earth orbits the Sun, the Sun is one of the Milky Way's stars, and the Milky Way is one galaxy among billions in the observable universe.
- Penguin Random House, [Cosmos by Carl Sagan](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/159730/cosmos-by-carl-sagan/). Publisher record used for the outward purchase link and for verifying that the book ranges across cosmic evolution, scientific history, life, and space exploration.
