# The 48 Laws of Power — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These primary and author-affiliated sources inform the chapter’s account
of *The 48 Laws of Power*. The chapter is original synthesis. Its diagrams are original
conceptual illustrations, not reproductions of the book’s expression or figures.

- Penguin Random House, [*The 48 Laws of Power*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/330912/the-48-laws-of-power-by-robert-greene/9781101042458/), Penguin Books. The publisher’s record identifies Robert Greene and Joost Elffers, describes the work as a distillation of historical material into 48 laws, and presents it as a guide to gaining, observing, or defending against control. It establishes the edition and is the reader link used by the page.
- Robert Greene, interview with Dorian Lynskey, [“Robert Greene on his 48 laws of power: ‘I’m not evil – I’m a realist’”](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2012/dec/03/robert-greene-48-laws-of-power). This is primary testimony from Greene about his intent and his view of the book as an account of social reality. It informs the chapter’s distinction between using the material as a lens and treating every tactic as a recommendation.

### Scope note

The source material makes broad historical and strategic claims, rather than testing a
universal model of organizational or interpersonal behavior. The diagrams do not present
historical data, and the exercises are the library’s own defensive, consent-respecting
applications. The caveat section deliberately names the book’s amoral and domination-oriented
framing, using the publisher’s language as evidence of what the work promotes.
