# Superintelligence — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. These author, university, and publisher sources support the chapter's
account of the book's scenario and vocabulary. The chapter's prose and diagrams are original
synthesis, not a reproduction of the book, its figures, or its expression.

- Nick Bostrom, [The Superintelligent Will: Motivation and Instrumental Rationality in Advanced Artificial Agents](https://nickbostrom.com/superintelligentwill.pdf), *Minds and Machines* 22 (2012): 71–85. Bostrom's paper develops the orthogonality and instrumental-convergence theses. It supports the chapter's distinction between competence and an objective, and its conditional explanation of why self-preservation, resources, information, and reduced interference can be useful intermediate aims across many final objectives.
- Nick Bostrom, [Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/publications/superintelligence-paths-dangers-strategies), Oxford Martin School, 2014. The university's book page identifies the 2014 publication, frames its question as whether initial conditions can make an intelligence explosion survivable, and names the control techniques and concepts considered by the book. It supports the chapter's scope and its claim that the book examines several possible routes and control approaches rather than offering a prediction of one fixed path.
- Oxford University Press, [Nick Bostrom on artificial intelligence](https://blog.oup.com/2014/09/interview-nick-bostrom-superintelligence/), 2014. In this author interview, Bostrom distinguishes specialized systems from general intelligence, explains why he sees potentially destructive subgoals as instrumental rather than necessarily malicious, and discusses the difficulty of a simple off switch. It informs the chapter's treatment of autonomy, access, and the difference between a narrow task tool and a broadly capable agent.
- Stuart Armstrong, Anders Sandberg, and Nick Bostrom, [Thinking Inside the Box: Using and Controlling an Oracle AI](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:ce99b73c-07f9-4607-b964-25a03206c6dd), *Minds and Machines* 22 (2012): 299–324. The Oxford repository record and abstract explain that even a system limited to answering questions may remain dangerous, while limited forms can be safer than unrestricted systems. This supports the chapter's caution that capability restrictions help but do not settle the control problem.
- Stuart Armstrong, Nick Bostrom, and Carl Shulman, [Racing to the Precipice: A Model of Artificial Intelligence Development](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/racing-to-the-precipice.pdf), 2013 technical report, later published in *AI & Society* (2016). The authors model competitive pressure that can reduce safety investment. It informs the chapter's treatment of an AI race as a coordination problem, not merely an individual engineering failure.
- Oxford University Press, [Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies](https://www.oupjapan.co.jp/en/node/14708?language=en). The publisher page confirms the book, author, and paperback edition details. It is the basis for the chapter's outward book link, which uses the corresponding Oxford Academic product page.
