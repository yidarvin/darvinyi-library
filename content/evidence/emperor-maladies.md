# The Emperor of All Maladies — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. These institutional sources support the chapter's account of cancer
biology, treatment resistance, clinical trials, prevention, screening, and disparities. The
chapter is an original synthesis of the book's ideas and current reference material, not a
reproduction of the book or its figures.

- National Cancer Institute, [Understanding Cancer](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding). NCI describes cancer as a collection of related diseases, not one disease, and explains why statistics also reveal disparities. This supports the chapter's central frame and its insistence on a broader measure of progress.
- National Cancer Institute, [What Is Cancer?](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer) and [The Genetics of Cancer](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics). These sources explain the roles of altered growth, tumor-suppressor, and DNA-repair genes; they also note that cells in one tumor can differ and that most cancer-related changes are acquired over time. They support Figure 87.1 and the account of cancer as both shared biology and varied disease.
- National Cancer Institute, [Why Do Cancer Treatments Stop Working?](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/research/drug-combo-resistance). NCI describes intrinsic and acquired resistance, molecular and microenvironmental mechanisms, and the rationale for combinations with different mechanisms. This informs the chapter's discussion and Figure 87.4, which is an original conceptual timeline rather than a depiction of source data.
- National Cancer Institute, [How Do Clinical Trials Work?](https://www.cancer.gov/research/participate/clinical-trials/how-trials-work) and [NCI clinical trials cooperative group](https://www.cancer.gov/publications/dictionaries/cancer-terms/def/nci-clinical-trials-cooperative-group). These sources explain phased trials, randomization's role in reducing bias, safeguards, and the multi-site cooperative groups supported by NCI. They ground the chapter's account of evidence as collective work.
- National Cancer Institute, [National Cancer Act of 1971](https://www.cancer.gov/about-nci/overview/history/national-cancer-act-1971) and [Milestones in Cancer Research and Discovery](https://www.cancer.gov/research/progress/250-years-milestones). These institutional histories support the chapter's account of cancer research as a public and organizational project, including the National Cancer Program and major biological and therapeutic milestones. They also support Figure 87.2's original historical synthesis: a shift from treating visible local disease, through systemic drug therapy and comparative trials, toward molecularly differentiated and immune-based approaches.
- National Cancer Institute, [Cancer Prevention Overview](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/patient-prevention-overview-pdq), [Cancer Screening](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/screening), and [Cancer Screening Overview](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/screening/patient-screening-overview-pdq). The sources support the distinction between prevention, early detection, and treatment, plus the possible harms of false results, overdiagnosis, and overtreatment. They inform the practice prompts and caveat without supplying individual medical advice.
- National Cancer Institute, [Tobacco](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/tobacco) and [About Cancer Health Disparities](https://www.cancer.gov/about-nci/organization/crchd/about-health-disparities). NCI identifies tobacco as a leading cause of cancer and cancer death and describes how socioeconomic conditions affect screening and outcomes. These sources ground the chapter's public-health and equity claims.
- Simon & Schuster, [The Emperor of All Maladies](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Emperor-of-All-Maladies/Siddhartha-Mukherjee/9781439181713). Used only for the page's outbound purchase link and publication details.
