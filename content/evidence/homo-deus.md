# Homo Deus — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. These sources inform the chapter's account of the book and its
caveats; the chapter is original synthesis rather than a reproduction of the book.

- Yuval Noah Harari, [Homo Deus](https://www.ynharari.com/book/homo-deus/). The author's book page describes the work as an examination of AI, genetic engineering, and the prospect that power and authority could shift toward networked algorithms. It also hosts an errata notice acknowledging factual errors discovered after publication.
- Yuval Noah Harari, [Homo Deus and the impact of digitalization on society](https://www.ynharari.com/homo-deus-impact-digitalization-society/). In this interview, Harari describes the book's "godlike" abilities as the capacity to engineer and create life, and frames the claims as a future-facing argument.
- Food and Agriculture Organization et al., [The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2025](https://www.fao.org/agrifood-economics/publications/detail/en/c/1740904/). The report says progress toward ending hunger remains uneven and the world is not on track to meet the 2030 hunger target.
- World Health Organization, [Draft fourteenth general programme of work, 2025–2028](https://apps.who.int/gb/ebwha/pdf_files/WHA77/A77_16-en.pdf). Its global-health overview reports that communicable diseases still kill millions of people each year, a useful correction to any reading of the book that treats plague as solved.
