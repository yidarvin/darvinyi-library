# Radical Candor — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These publisher and author-affiliated primary sources inform this
chapter's account of *Radical Candor*. The chapter is original synthesis. Its diagrams are
original conceptual SVGs, not copies of the book's prose, illustrations, or cover.

- Kim Scott, [*Radical Candor*](https://www.radicalcandor.com/the-book), Radical Candor, 2017. The author's book page identifies the book and provides the reader link used by this page.
- St. Martin's Press, [*Radical Candor: Be a Kick-Ass Boss Without Losing Your Humanity*](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781427283139/radicalcandorbeakickassbosswithoutlosingyourhumanity/), 2017. The publisher record confirms the original edition and frames the book as guidance for leadership, communication, and results.
- Radical Candor, [What is Radical Candor?](https://www.radicalcandor.com/blog/what-is-radical-candor). The official explainer defines the framework through personal care and direct challenge, and distinguishes its four regions. It supports the chapter's central matrix and its distinction from cruelty.
- Kim Scott, [How to get and give feedback](https://www.radicalcandor.com/blog/how-to-give-and-receive-feedback). Scott's official guidance presents an order of operations: seek feedback, offer it, check how it landed, and invite it more broadly. It informs the chapter's treatment of feedback as an exchange rather than a one-way delivery.
- Kim Scott, [A HIP approach to feedback](https://www.radicalcandor.com/blog/a-hip-approach-to-feedback). This author-affiliated practice guide emphasizes humility, helpfulness, timeliness, direct conversation where possible, public praise, private criticism, and keeping guidance about work rather than personality. It supports the chapter's practical exercises.
- Kim Scott, [Avoid personalizing feedback](https://www.radicalcandor.com/blog/dont-personalize-feedback). The official guidance recommends describing observable behavior and impact instead of making character judgments. It supports the chapter's work-versus-person distinction.
- Kim Scott, [Gauge your feedback](https://www.radicalcandor.com/blog/how-to-gauge-your-feedback). This source emphasizes that a message must be checked at the listener's end, not assumed effective because the speaker intended it well. It informs the chapter's feedback-calibration loop.

### Scope note

The framework is a management and communication practice, not evidence that direct feedback
is safe or sufficient in every workplace. A manager's power, organizational incentives,
discrimination, retaliation, workload, and formal employment processes all shape what a
person can safely say and hear. The diagrams and exercises are original applications of the
framework, not evidence of universal outcomes.
