# The Demon-Haunted World — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. These sources support the chapter's factual claims and scope. The
chapter's prose and diagrams are original synthesis, not a reproduction of the book, its
figures, or its expression.

- Library of Congress, [The demon-haunted world: science as a candle in the dark: draft](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mss85590.004/?sp=13&st=list). The archive describes this as a full 1994 draft of Sagan's 1995 book and identifies its focus on separating science from pseudoscience, including critical thinking, skepticism, UFOs, ESP, witchcraft, and faith healing. It grounds the chapter's scope without relying on the book's wording or structure.
- Carl Sagan Institute at Cornell, [Misattribution will often carry us to quotes that never were](https://carlsaganinstitute.cornell.edu/news/misattribution-will-often-carry-us-quotes-never-were). The Institute identifies the "Baloney Detection Kit" as a chapter 12 framework and gives independent confirmation, source checking, and resistance to authority as relevant habits. Figure 130.6 is an original decision flow built from those underlying habits, not a recreation of a book figure.
- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, [Scientific Methods and Knowledge](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547541/). The report describes science as a communal effort that uses hypotheses, evidence, reasoning, communication, checking, and revision. It supports the chapter's distinction between a single persuasive claim and durable confidence accumulated through scrutiny.
- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, [Understanding Reproducibility and Replicability](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547546/). Supports the claim that independent work with new data can strengthen a result, while terminology and exact methods vary by field. The chapter deliberately does not present replication as a mechanical guarantee of truth.
- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, [How Science Works](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK621586/). Supports the caveat that peer review alone cannot guarantee correct conclusions and that broad acceptance grows from repeated, varied testing. This informs the note against turning skepticism into either automatic disbelief or deference to a single institution.
- Penguin Random House, [The Demon-Haunted World](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/159731/the-demon-haunted-world-by-carl-sagan/). The publisher's page confirms the current edition and supplies the chapter's outward purchase link. Its description also supports the book's concern with public reasoning, technology, pseudoscience, and democratic life.
