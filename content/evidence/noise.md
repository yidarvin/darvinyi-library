# Noise — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These primary sources and original research records support the
chapter's account of unwanted variability in judgment, noise audits, and decision hygiene.
The chapter is an original synthesis of the book's ideas and source material, not a
reproduction of its text or figures.

- Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony, and Cass R. Sunstein, [*Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment*](https://www.littlebrown.com/titles/daniel-kahneman/noise/9780316451406/). The publisher's direct book page identifies the 2021 book and supplies the chapter's purchase path. Its description frames the subject as differing judgments on cases that should be handled alike across medicine, law, forecasting, personnel selection, and other fields.
- Daniel Kahneman, Andrew M. Rosenfield, Linnea Gandhi, and Tom Blaser, ["Noise: How to Overcome the High, Hidden Cost of Inconsistent Decision Making"](https://kahneman.scholar.princeton.edu/publications), *Harvard Business Review*, 2016. Kahneman's official publication record identifies the article and its authors. It supports the chapter's central distinction between systematic error and inconsistency in organizational decisions.
- Daniel Kahneman, Andrew M. Rosenfield, Linnea Gandhi, and Tom Blaser, ["Reducing Noise in Decision Making"](https://kahneman.scholar.princeton.edu/publications), *Harvard Business Review*, 2016. The same primary author publication record lists this companion article. It supports treating noise reduction as a process-design task rather than a claim about any one evaluator's character.
- Daniel Kahneman, Dan Lovallo, and Olivier Sibony, ["A Structured Approach to Strategic Decisions"](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/store/a-structured-approach-to-strategic-decisions), *MIT Sloan Management Review*, 2019. The publisher's article page describes a disciplined, sequential approach for evaluative strategic decisions. It grounds the chapter's practices of decomposing a broad decision, structuring the sequence, and leaving a deliberate place for judgment.
- Hasnain, Onishi, and Elstein, ["Inter-rater agreement in judging errors in diagnostic reasoning"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15189257/), *Medical Education*, 2004. Two independent physician judges rated 54 clinical work-ups. The study found strong agreement where scoring rules were explicit and weaker agreement where judges had to rely on judgment alone. This supports the cautious claim that explicit criteria can improve consistency in a defined task; it does not prove that one rubric solves every clinical decision.
- Quinn and colleagues, ["Interobserver variability studies in diagnostic imaging: a methodological systematic review"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37399082/), *British Journal of Radiology*, 2023. The review examined 79 studies and documents how observer variability is measured, while warning that design and interpretation vary. It supports the chapter's caveat that an audit must use representative cases and suitable measures, rather than treating a single statistic as a final diagnosis.
- Olivier Sibony, ["Sounding the alarm on system noise"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/sounding-the-alarm-on-system-noise), interview, 2021. Sibony explains decision hygiene as preventive procedures and identifies independent judgments as a practical technique. This author interview supports the practice prompt's emphasis on private assessments before group discussion; the chapter does not claim that aggregation guarantees correctness.
