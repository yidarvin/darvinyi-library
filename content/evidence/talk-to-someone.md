# Maybe You Should Talk to Someone — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These primary author and publisher sources inform this chapter's
original synthesis of *Maybe You Should Talk to Someone*. Its prose and conceptual SVGs are
new work. They do not reproduce the book's text, figures, or cover.

- Lori Gottlieb, [*Maybe You Should Talk to Someone* discussion guide](https://lorigottlieb.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/MYSTTS-discussion-guide.pdf), 2019. Gottlieb's guide describes the book's dual clinician-and-patient perspective, identifies the five narratives it follows, and frames the work around the stories people tell themselves, change, meaning, loss, and connection.
- Lori Gottlieb, ["How changing your story can change your life"](https://www.ted.com/talks/lori_gottlieb_how_changing_your_story_can_change_your_life), TED, 2019. In her own account of the book's premise, Gottlieb explains that people narrate from a partial point of view and that changing a narrative can change what becomes possible.
- Lori Gottlieb, ["How changing your story can change your life" transcript](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/how-to-be-a-better-human/how-changing-your-story-can-change-your-life-w-lori-gottlieb-transcript), *How to Be a Better Human*, 2023. In this interview, Gottlieb discusses the therapeutic relationship, recurring relational dynamics, the loss within desired change, and maintenance as a non-linear part of change.
- Penguin Random House, [Lori Gottlieb](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/authors/239394/lori-gottlieb/). The publisher's author page confirms the book's title, author, and publisher link used on this page.

### Scope note

This chapter describes a memoir-informed view of psychotherapy, not a diagnosis or a universal
treatment protocol. A reflective exercise can help make a pattern clearer, but it cannot make a
relationship safe, resolve abuse, or replace qualified care. For urgent mental-health support or
immediate risk of self-harm, contact local emergency services or a crisis service.
