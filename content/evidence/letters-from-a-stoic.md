# Letters from a Stoic — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. The primary text was consulted in Richard M. Gummere's public-domain
translation of Seneca's *Moral Letters to Lucilius*. The chapter is original synthesis and its
diagrams are original conceptual illustrations, not reproductions of the text or any source figure.

- Seneca, [Letter 1, "On Saving Time"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_1). This primary letter grounds the chapter's treatment of time as a finite possession that can be wasted through neglect and delay.
- Seneca, [Letter 18, "On Festivals and Fasting"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_18). Seneca's advice to rehearse simple living and hardship informs the account of voluntary simplicity as preparation, not an achievement claim about involuntary poverty.
- Seneca, [Letter 24, "On Despising Death"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_24), and [Letter 61, "On Meeting Death Cheerfully"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_61). These primary letters inform the themes of examined fear, mortality, and treating a day as a whole life without reproducing their language or order.
- Seneca, [Letter 47, "On Master and Slave"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_47). This primary letter supports the chapter's discussion of Seneca's critique of cruel treatment, shared human vulnerability, and the limits of a critique that did not reject Roman slavery itself.
- Penguin Random House, [*Letters from a Stoic*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/317438/letters-from-a-stoic-by-seneca-translated-with-an-introduction-by-robin-campbell-cover-illustrated-by-coralie-bickford-smith/). The publisher identifies the Robin Campbell translation and its 1969 publication. This page is also the chapter's outbound reader link.
