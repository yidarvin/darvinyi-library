# Getting Past No — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These author, publisher, and institutional sources support the
chapter's account of William Ury's approach to difficult negotiation. The chapter is
an original synthesis. Its prose and diagrams do not reproduce the book's text,
figures, or cover.

- William Ury, [*Getting Past No*](https://www.williamury.com/getting-past-no/), official author site. Ury describes the book as a strategy for turning adversarial negotiations into cooperative ones, including staying in control under pressure, defusing hostility, discovering interests, responding to tactics, and using power to return to the table.
- William Ury, ["Negotiation starts from the inside out"](https://www.williamury.com/negotiation-starts-from-the-inside-out/), official author site, 2024. In this interview, Ury describes gaining calm and perspective before engaging, attending to the other side's concerns without abandoning one's own, and making an agreement attractive enough for the other side to choose it.
- Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School, ["Try Skills-Based Strategies First"](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/try-skills-based-strategies-first/), 2012. This institutional guide, explicitly adapted from the book, supports the chapter's sequence of listening, pausing rather than reacting, finding joint interests, and making a face-saving option available.
- Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School, ["When Dealing with Difficult People, Try a Complementary Approach"](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/dealing-with-difficult-people-daily/when-dealing-with-difficult-people-try-a-complementary-approach/), 2023. The article supports the limited claim that reacting in kind tends to escalate an already difficult negotiation, and that a deliberate pause can create room for a more objective response.

### Scope note

This is a method for a negotiable conflict, not a duty to remain in every conflict.
It cannot equalize coercive power, make an unsafe person safe, or replace legal advice,
organizational safeguards, or a firm boundary. A credible alternative and outside support
may be the wiser response when cooperation is not available.
