# Make Time — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These official sources support the chapter's original account of
the book's four-part daily practice, its emphasis on attention and energy, and its
flexible, experimental stance. The chapter is an original synthesis of ideas, not a
reproduction of the book's text or figures.

- Jake Knapp and John Zeratsky, [Make Time](https://maketime.blog/). The authors' official
  site identifies the four parts of the framework: choose a daily priority, reduce
  distraction, support energy, and adjust through reflection. It supports the chapter's
  account of the model and its daily cadence.
- Jake Knapp and John Zeratsky, [How Make Time Works](https://maketime.blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Make-Time-Introduction-and-How-It-Works.pdf). The authors' official excerpt describes
  the method as a menu of small experiments rather than a fixed prescription. It grounds
  the chapter's insistence on changing one workable condition at a time.
- Jake Knapp and John Zeratsky, [Official worksheets](https://maketime.blog/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Make-Time-Worksheets.pdf). The worksheet asks readers to choose a Highlight and
  record focus, energy, and tactics tried. It supports the chapter's short end-of-day
  review practice.
- Penguin Random House, [Make Time by Jake Knapp and John Zeratsky](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/564727/make-time-by-jake-knapp-and-john-zeratsky/). The publisher's page confirms the
  2018 publication and frames the book as small, customizable changes to everyday
  defaults. It is also the chapter's qualifying outbound book link.
