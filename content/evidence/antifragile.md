# Antifragile — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. These primary and publisher sources inform the chapter's account of the
book, its framework, and its limits. The chapter is original synthesis and its diagrams are
original conceptual illustrations, not reproductions of the book or any source figure.

- Nassim Nicholas Taleb, [Understanding is a Poor Substitute for Convexity (Antifragility)](https://fooledbyrandomness.com/ConvexityScience.pdf), 2012. Taleb's paper states the payoff asymmetry behind his use of antifragility: trials need small or harmless errors and potentially larger gains. It supports the chapter's treatment of optionality, bounded experimentation, and the distinction between useful learning and ruinous exposure.
- Nassim Nicholas Taleb, [(Anti)Fragility and Convex Responses in Medicine](https://fooledbyrandomness.com/medicine.pdf). This working paper defines antifragility locally through convex response and fragility through its opposite, then explicitly frames dose and intervention effects as nonlinear. It informs the chapter's caution that response depends on range and domain rather than on a universal prescription to add stress.
- Penguin Random House, [Antifragile](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/176227/antifragile-by-nassim-nicholas-taleb/hardcover/). The publisher describes the book's fragile, resilient, and antifragile distinction; its span across risk, innovation, policy, finance, and medicine; and the 2012 Random House edition. The page is also used for this chapter's outbound reader link.
