# The Culture Code — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. This chapter is an original synthesis of Daniel Coyle's account of
culture-building. It does not reproduce the book's prose, stories, figures, or cover. Its
diagrams are original conceptual SVGs made from the library's fixed diagram vocabulary.

- Daniel Coyle, [*The Culture Code*](https://danielcoyle.com/the-culture-code/). The author's book page frames strong culture as three deliberate skills: safety, vulnerability, and purpose. It grounds the chapter's scope and three-part account.
- Daniel Coyle, ["An Excerpt From The Culture Code"](https://danielcoyle.com/excerpt-culture-code/). Coyle describes building safety, sharing vulnerability, and establishing purpose as supports for coordinated action. It informs the chapter's claim that the skills reinforce rather than replace one another.
- Daniel Coyle, ["A Q&A About The Culture Code"](https://danielcoyle.com/q-and-a-culture-code/). Coyle describes belonging cues, work-specific fallibility, the sharing of uncomfortable truths, and purpose communicated through stories and other signals. It supports the chapter's treatment of culture as repeated behavior.
- Daniel Coyle, ["How to Think About Culture"](https://danielcoyle.com/2018/02/06/how-to-think-about-culture/). Coyle emphasizes the reinforcing relationship among safety, shared vulnerability, and story-based direction. It informs the final systems diagram.
- Bantam, [*The Culture Code*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/235200/the-culture-code-by-daniel-coyle/9780804176989/), 2018. The publisher record confirms the 2018 edition and describes the book as practical guidance for cohesion, cooperation, trust, and positive change across groups.

### Scope note

The source offers case-led management guidance, not a controlled estimate of the effect of any
single ritual. The diagrams show conceptual relationships, not measured results or copies of
source figures. Psychological safety and productive accountability depend on real protections,
distribution of power, workload, and organizational policy. The practice section therefore
treats culture as work on interactions and systems, not as a substitute for changing harmful
conditions.
