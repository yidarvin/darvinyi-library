# Capital in the Twenty-First Century — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. This chapter is an original synthesis of Piketty's argument about
wealth, income, and long-run concentration. It does not reproduce the book's prose, tables,
or figures. Its diagrams are new conceptual SVGs built from the library vocabulary.

- Thomas Piketty, [About *Capital in the Twenty-First Century*](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.p20151060), *American Economic Review* 105(5), 2015. In this author-written follow-up, Piketty identifies three long-run facts from the book and explicitly says that it clarifies the role of r > g in the wealth-inequality analysis. It supports the chapter's framing of the formula as one part of an argument about wealth distribution, capital-income ratios, and taxation rather than a complete prediction rule.
- Thomas Piketty, [publications and data archive](https://piketty.pse.ens.fr/en/publications-en). The author's publication record links the book-related follow-up and the historical work on wealth-income ratios with Gabriel Zucman. It informs the chapter's distinction between wealth as a stock and income as an annual flow, and its historical rather than chapter-by-chapter organization.
- World Inequality Lab, [World Inequality Report 2018, Part IV](https://wir2018.wid.world/part-4.html). The report identifies unequal savings rates, different rates of return, income inequality, and tax progressivity as interacting drivers of wealth concentration. It explicitly warns that there was nothing inevitable about the top share's post-1980 rise. This supports Key Ideas 3 through 5 and the scope note in the caveat.
- World Inequality Database, [What’s new about wealth inequality in the world?](https://wid.world/news-article/whats-new-about-wealth-inequality-in-the-world/), 2023. The database's update reports that its wealth series extend through 2022 and explains that wealth measurement remains incomplete and subject to revision. It also describes its combined use of national accounts, fiscal records, surveys, and wealth rankings. This informs the chapter's measurement caveat.
- Harvard University Press, [*Capital in the Twenty-First Century*](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674979857/capital-in-the-twenty-first-century). Publisher record and outbound reader link for Thomas Piketty's book.

### Scope note

The diagram labeled r > g is intentionally illustrative, not a recreation of a source chart
or a claim about a universal numerical spread. The chapter treats r > g as a mechanism that
can amplify concentration when ownership, savings, and returns differ, while recognizing
that the observed distribution depends on institutions, shocks, and measurement choices.
