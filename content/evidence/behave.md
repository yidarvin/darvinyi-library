# Behave — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. These sources inform this chapter's original synthesis of
*Behave*. The prose and conceptual SVGs are new work. They do not reproduce the
book's text, figures, or cover.

- Robert M. Sapolsky, [*Behave*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/311787/behave-by-robert-m-sapolsky/9780735222786/), Penguin Random House, 2017. The publisher confirms the edition, author, and the book's cross-disciplinary examination of human behavior.
- Robert M. Sapolsky, [author interview](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/authors/2147388), Penguin Random House. Sapolsky describes the book's theme as behavior being contingent on biology, informing the chapter's focus on explanation rather than a single moral label.
- Lars Schwabe et al., ["The stressed prefrontal cortex and goal-directed behaviour"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22101494/), *Experimental Brain Research*, 2011. In a controlled study of 48 healthy volunteers, acute psychosocial stress was associated with larger task-switching costs during the measured post-stress intervals. It supports the narrow claim that recent stress can affect flexible control, not a universal prediction of behavior.
- Erik L. Knight et al., ["The causal effect of testosterone on men's competitive behavior is moderated by basal cortisol and cues to an opponent's status"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35201818/), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2022. This experiment with 115 men manipulated testosterone before a competition and found effects contingent on cortisol and status cues. It supports the chapter's rejection of one-hormone stories.
- Carsten K. W. De Dreu et al., ["The neuropeptide oxytocin regulates parochial altruism in intergroup conflict among humans"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20538951/), *Science*, 2010. In three double-blind placebo-controlled experiments with male participants, oxytocin increased in-group trust and cooperation and defensive behavior toward a competing out-group. This is a context-specific result, not evidence that any hormone determines group behavior in general.

### Scope note

This chapter describes a framework for thinking about behavior. It is not a tool for diagnosing
people, predicting violence, assigning legal responsibility, or replacing clinical assessment.
Biological, developmental, social, and cultural explanations can all matter without settling an
individual case on their own.
