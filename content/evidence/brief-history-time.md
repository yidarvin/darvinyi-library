# A Brief History of Time — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. These sources inform the chapter's account of the book and its
caveats; the chapter is original synthesis rather than a reproduction of the book.

- NASA, [General Relativity and the Nature of Spacetime](https://science.nasa.gov/astrophysics/programs/physics-of-the-cosmos/general-relativity-and-the-nature-of-spacetime/). NASA describes general relativity as a fundamental theory expected to be incomplete because it lacks a quantum foundation, and identifies black-hole environments as strong-field tests.
- NASA, [Big Bang and the Evolution of the Universe](https://science.nasa.gov/astrophysics/programs/physics-of-the-cosmos/big-bang-and-the-evolution-of-the-universe/). The agency explains that the cosmic microwave background was released about 380,000 years after the Big Bang and is now observed as a 2.7 K glow stretched by cosmic expansion.
- European Space Agency, [Planck and the cosmic microwave background](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Planck/Planck_and_the_cosmic_microwave_background). ESA explains why the early universe was opaque, how neutral atoms let light travel freely, and how expansion redshifted that first light into microwaves.
- NASA, [What Are Black Holes?](https://www.nasa.gov/universe/what-are-black-holes/). NASA defines the event horizon as the boundary beyond which escape requires exceeding light speed, and notes both the 2019 Event Horizon Telescope image and gravitational-wave detections.
- NASA, [10 Questions You Might Have About Black Holes](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/10-questions-you-might-have-about-black-holes/). The agency summarizes Hawking radiation as a theoretical proposal by which black holes lose energy and, in principle, eventually evaporate.
- Penguin Random House, [A Brief History of Time](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/116467/a-brief-history-of-time-by-stephen-hawking/). Publisher page used for the reader link to the complete work.
