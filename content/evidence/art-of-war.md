# The Art of War — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. The received text was consulted through the Chinese Text Project,
alongside a current publisher edition. This chapter is original synthesis: its prose reorganizes
the ideas by decision problem, and its diagrams are original conceptual illustrations rather
than reproductions of a translation, commentary, or historical figure.

- Sunzi, [*The Art of War*, overview](https://ctext.org/art-of-war), and [“Laying Plans”](https://ctext.org/art-of-war/laying-plans/ens). The Chinese Text Project presents the received Chinese text alongside Lionel Giles's English translation and identifies the thirteen sections. “Laying Plans” frames deliberation around comparative conditions including shared purpose, timing, ground, leadership, and organization. It supports the chapter's emphasis on inspecting conditions before commitment and coordinating a group before action.
- Sunzi, [“Waging War”](https://ctext.org/art-of-war/waging-war/ens), and [“Attack by Stratagem”](https://ctext.org/art-of-war/attack-by-stratagem). These primary-text sections stress the burden of prolonged campaigning and prefer preserving an opponent's state and forces over destructive victory. They ground the chapter's claim that a favorable outcome includes retained capacity and that force is not the automatic first choice.
- Sunzi, [“Weak Points and Strong”](https://ctext.org/art-of-war/weak-points-and-strong), and [“Energy”](https://ctext.org/art-of-war/energy/ens). The text ties effectiveness to comparative knowledge, adaptation to the opposing side and terrain, concentration, and clear signs and signals. It supports the chapter's accounts of changing the situation, learning what matters, and making coordinated action possible.
- Macmillan Collector's Library, [*The Art of War*, translated and edited by Jonathan Clements](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781509827954/theartofwar/). The publisher identifies this 2017 English edition and is the chapter's outbound reader link.

### Attribution boundary

The page's iceberg, flow, spectrum, comparison, and process-loop diagrams are this library's
teaching devices. The source does not supply those diagrams, nor does it present a single modern
framework called “strategic advantage.” The page uses "preserve capacity," "find an opening," and
"learn before committing" as plain-language synthesis of the recorded passages. It does not treat
the work as general permission for deception, coercion, or treating ordinary relationships as war.
