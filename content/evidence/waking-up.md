# Waking Up — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. This chapter is an original synthesis of *Waking Up*'s central
arguments and practice themes. It does not reproduce the book's prose, instructions, figures,
or cover art. Its diagrams are original conceptual illustrations using the library's fixed SVG
vocabulary.

- Sam Harris, [*Waking Up*](https://www.samharris.org/books/waking-up). The author's official
  page identifies the book as a 2014 inquiry into spirituality without religion, consciousness,
  meditation, and the changeable sense of a separate self. It supports the chapter's account of
  secular spiritual practice and the experiential inquiry into selfhood.
- Sam Harris, ["WAKING UP: Chapter One"](https://www.samharris.org/podcasts/making-sense-episodes/chapter-one).
  Harris describes mindfulness meditation as simple to state but difficult to perform, and as a
  technique for waking up. This informs the chapter's restrained, beginner-oriented practice
  cards.
- Norman A. S. Farb et al., ["Attending to the present: mindfulness meditation reveals distinct
  neural modes of self-reference"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18985137/), *Social Cognitive
  and Affective Neuroscience* (2007), DOI: 10.1093/scan/nsm030. This primary fMRI study compared
  narrative and present-centered experiential focus. It found different patterns of activity and
  connectivity, with more pronounced changes after an eight-week mindfulness course. It supports
  the chapter's careful distinction between narrative self-focus and present experience, not a
  claim that meditation proves a philosophical theory of self.
- Yanli Lin, Courtney P. Callahan, and Jason S. Moser, ["A mind full of self"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29886175/),
  *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* (2018), DOI: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.06.007. This review
  describes evidence that mindfulness training can shift self-reference toward present-centered
  experience and has moderate effects on internalizing symptoms. It informs the caveat's modest
  statement of the evidence.
- Simon & Schuster, [*Waking Up* official publisher page](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Waking-Up/Sam-Harris/9781451636024).
  The publisher describes the book as a guide to meditation informed by neuroscience and
  psychology. It is the chapter's outbound reader link.
