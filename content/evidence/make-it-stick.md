# Make It Stick — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These primary research and author-associated sources inform the chapter's
account of retrieval practice, distributed practice, interleaving, and the book's scope. The
chapter is original synthesis, and every diagram is an original conceptual illustration rather
than a reproduction of a book or research figure.

- Brown, Roediger, and McDaniel, [Make It Stick](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674729018/make-it-stick), Harvard University Press. Publisher record for the 2014 book, author credits, and reader link.
- Karpicke and Blunt, [Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/), *Science* (2011). In experiments using science texts, retrieval practice produced stronger meaningful learning than elaborative study with concept mapping, including on inference questions. This informs the chapter's account of recall as a learning activity rather than merely an assessment.
- Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted, and Rohrer, [Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/), *Psychological Bulletin* (2006). This quantitative review reports that the interval yielding the best final retention varies jointly with the intended retention interval. It grounds the chapter's rejection of a single universal spacing schedule.
- Kornell and Bjork, [Learning Concepts and Categories: Is Spacing the “Enemy of Induction”?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18578849/), *Psychological Science* (2008). The paper studies category learning through examples and informs the chapter's treatment of interleaving as practice in discriminating among related categories.
- Retrieval Practice, [Make It Stick](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/make-it-stick). This author-associated resource describes the book as a synthesis of cognitive-science research and identifies retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, errors, and metacognition as major themes. It supports the chapter's framing while the research claims above remain grounded in their original studies.

### Scope note

The recorded research supports the chapter's retrieval, distributed-practice, and category-discrimination claims separately. It does not record an interaction result requiring retrieval, spacing, and interleaving to be used together. The chapter therefore presents them as complementary moves, not a three-way causal intersection. Its flexible-review and elaboration prompts are explicitly attributed to the book's practical recommendations, rather than presented as effects established by the recorded studies.
