# Originals — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. This chapter is an original synthesis of Adam Grant's argument about
questioning defaults, generating and judging ideas, using a bounded incubation period, taking
responsible experiments, and making room for genuine dissent. It does not reproduce the book's
prose, stories, or figures. Its diagrams are original conceptual SVGs built from the library
vocabulary.

- Adam Grant, [*Originals*](https://adamgrant.net/book/originals/). Grant's official book page says the book concerns championing new ideas and fighting groupthink, including recognizing ideas, speaking up, building coalitions, timing action, and managing fear and doubt. It grounds the chapter's overall scope and outbound reader link.
- Adam Grant, ["The surprising habits of original thinkers"](https://www.ted.com/talks/adam_grant_the_surprising_habits_of_original_thinkers?view=transcript), TED2016, February 2016. Grant defines originals as people who generate new ideas and act to put them into the world. The talk also supports the chapter's rejection of the lone-perfect-idea myth: many attempts include failures and weak ideas.
- Adam Grant, ["Six secrets to true originality"](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/McKinsey/Business%20Functions/Organization/Our%20Insights/Six%20secrets%20to%20true%20originality/Six-secrets-to-true-originality.pdf), McKinsey, 2016. In this author interview, Grant discusses generating a large variety of ideas, seeking feedback from informed peers rather than relying solely on creators or managers, breadth and depth, genuine dissent, and a bounded delay that allows incubation. This supports Key Ideas 2 through 4 and 6.
- Jihae Shin and Adam M. Grant, ["When Putting Work Off Pays Off: The Curvilinear Relationship between Procrastination and Creativity"](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2018.1471), *Academy of Management Journal*, 2021. The abstract reports a proposed curvilinear relationship: moderate procrastination may support creativity when people have intrinsic motivation and opportunity to generate ideas. It supports the chapter's limited claim about an incubation window, not a prescription to delay routine work or deadlines.
- Adam Grant, ["How to Build a Culture of Originality"](https://hbr.org/2016/03/how-to-build-a-culture-of-originality), *Harvard Business Review*, 2016. Grant frames originality as broadly available with opportunity and support. It informs the chapter's focus on organization-level conditions, while the practical exercises and diagrams remain this library's own synthesis.

### Scope note

The diagrams use conceptual relative sizes and a schematic curve, not measured results or
recreations of source figures. The chapter treats novelty as a proposal for testing rather than
an inherent virtue. Its practical guidance adds explicit attention to reversibility, consent,
and who bears downside risk, which are ethical guardrails for applying the book's ideas.
