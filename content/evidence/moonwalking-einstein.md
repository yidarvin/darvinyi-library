# Moonwalking with Einstein — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. These publisher and primary-research sources inform the chapter's
account of Joshua Foer's project, the method of loci, and the method's limits. The chapter is
original synthesis, and its diagrams are original conceptual illustrations rather than copies
of a book or research figure.

- Joshua Foer, [*Moonwalking with Einstein*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/301277/moonwalking-with-einstein-by-joshua-foer/), Penguin Random House. The publisher record identifies the 2011 book as Foer's yearlong effort to improve his memory under the guidance of memory athletes, and is the source for the reader link.
- Dresler et al., [Mnemonic Training Reshapes Brain Networks to Support Superior Memory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5439266/), *Neuron* (2017). In a controlled study, mnemonic-naive participants received six weeks of method-of-loci training, working-memory training, or no training. The chapter's claim that the technique is practiced rather than innate is grounded in this training design, not in an assumption that athletes are born different.
- Wagner et al., [Durable Memories and Efficient Neural Coding Through Mnemonic Training Using the Method of Loci](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7929507/), *Science Advances* (2021). This primary study compared memory athletes with matched controls and separately tested six weeks of loci training in novices. It supports the chapter's description of a practiced route as useful for ordered word-list recall and of effects measured after training.
- McDaniel and Einstein, [Bizarre Imagery, Interference, and Distinctiveness](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2522138/), *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* (1989). Experiments found a retrieval advantage for bizarre over common images in one interference condition. It informs the chapter's restrained advice to make a scene distinctive, without treating strangeness as a universal law.
- Twomey and Kroneisen, [The Effectiveness of the Loci Method as a Mnemonic Device](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33535926/), *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology* (2021). This meta-analysis of 13 randomized trials reports a medium average effect and flags risks of experimental bias. It informs the caveat that the technique has a useful evidence base but does not establish broad transfer to every memory problem.

### Scope note

The recorded studies test specific recall tasks, chiefly lists and serial order, after structured
training. They do not establish that a memory palace improves understanding, judgment,
attention, prospective memory, or every kind of everyday forgetfulness. The page therefore
presents the method as a deliberate encoding and retrieval tool for selected material, not as a
replacement for comprehension, notes, calendars, sleep, or medical care.
