# The Sixth Extinction — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. These sources inform this chapter's original synthesis of
*The Sixth Extinction*. Its prose and conceptual diagrams are newly written and do not
reproduce the book's text, figures, sequence, or cover art.

- Elizabeth Kolbert, [*The Sixth Extinction*](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780805099799/thesixthextinction/), Henry Holt and Company, 2014. The publisher page establishes the work and its field-reporting scope. This chapter organizes its explanation by mechanisms and decisions rather than following the book's chapter order.
- Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services, [*Global Assessment Report: Summary for Policymakers*](https://files.ipbes.net/ipbes-web-prod-public-files/ipbes_global_assessment_report_summary_for_policymakers.pdf), 2019. The assessment identifies land and sea-use change, direct exploitation, climate change, pollution, and invasive alien species as direct drivers of change in nature. It informs the system map in Figure 128.3 and the chapter's account of interacting pressures.
- Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services, [*Invasive Alien Species Assessment*](https://ict.ipbes.net/ipbes-ict-guide/data-and-knowledge-management/citations-of-ipbes-assessments/invasive-alien-species-assessment), 2023. This primary assessment documents the consequences of human-mediated species introductions and their management. It supports Figure 128.4's original causal sequence, not any species-specific claim or borrowed figure.
- International Union for Conservation of Nature, [Red List summary statistics](https://nrl.iucnredlist.org/resources/summary-statistics). IUCN explains the categories, assessment coverage, uncertainty from Data Deficient species, and why its list is not a complete assessment of all biodiversity. It grounds the distinction between confirmed extinction, elevated risk, and incomplete measurement in the chapter's caveat.
- International Union for Conservation of Nature, [Red List Index](https://nrl.iucnredlist.org/assessment/red-list-index). The index isolates genuine changes in extinction risk from expanding assessment effort. It supports the instruction to interpret changing Red List totals with care.

### Scope note

This page is a conceptual distillation of an environmental-history argument, not a current
species assessment, legal determination, ecological impact analysis, or conservation plan.
Questions about a particular species or project require current local evidence and the relevant
scientific and regulatory expertise.
