# Measure What Matters — research notes

Verified 2026-07-27. These publisher and author-affiliated primary sources inform the
chapter's account of *Measure What Matters* and its OKR framework. The chapter is original
synthesis, and every diagram is an original conceptual illustration rather than a
reproduction of the book or any source figure.

- John Doerr, [Measure What Matters](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/546304/measure-what-matters-by-john-doerr-foreword-by-larry-page/), Portfolio, 2018. The publisher record establishes the edition, author, publication date, and the book's account of objectives, measurable key results, transparency, focus, and coordination. It is also the reader link used by the page.
- What Matters, [What’s a Good OKR?](https://www.whatmatters.com/okrs-explained/good-example-okrs). John Doerr's official OKR training explains the division between an objective and its key results, and the criteria that results be specific, time-bound, measurable, and verifiable. It informs the chapter's proof-versus-activity distinction and its example design.
- What Matters, [How Many OKRs Should You Have?](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/how-many-okrs-to-have). This author-affiliated guidance recommends a deliberately small number of objectives and results, discusses transparency, and describes a balance between top-down and bottom-up goal setting. It supports the chapter's treatment of focus and alignment as operating choices.
- What Matters, [Inputs, Outputs, and Outcomes](https://www.whatmatters.com/okrs-explained/input-output-outcome-key-results). The source distinguishes actions, outputs, and outcomes, and explains why different kinds of key results can work together. It informs the chapter's caution against mistaking a task list for proof of change.
- What Matters, [Committed vs. Aspirational OKRs](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/committed-aspirational-okrs-examples-difference). This official guidance distinguishes goals that require dependable delivery from goals intended to stretch a team, and emphasizes regular check-ins. It supports the chapter's distinction between reliability and deliberate experimentation.
- What Matters, [OKRs vs. KPIs](https://www.whatmatters.com/okrs-explained/kpis-and-okrs). The source treats OKRs as cycle priorities that can elevate a health metric when improvement is needed, not as a replacement for the broader KPI dashboard. It supports the chapter's caveat about measures outside the selected goal.
- What Matters, [Why You Should Divorce Compensation From OKRs](https://www.whatmatters.com/articles/laszlo-bock-divorce-compensation-from-okrs). The source explains why ambitious goals can be distorted when directly tied to compensation. It informs the chapter's warning against using an aspirational score as a blunt individual performance formula.

### Scope note

This page describes a goal-setting and review framework, not a demonstrated universal causal
formula for organizational performance. The author-affiliated sources present operational
guidance and examples. They do not establish that OKRs choose the right strategy, prevent
metric distortion, or work without capable management, good information, and room to change
course. The exercises and diagrams are original applications of the framework.
