# The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. These primary institutional sources support the chapter's account of
Henrietta Lacks, HeLa cells, genome-data governance, consent, and biospecimen privacy. The
chapter is an original synthesis of the book's ideas and source material, not a reproduction of
the book or its figures.

- National Institutes of Health, [NIH-Lacks Family Agreement](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/dms/privacy/nih-lacks-family-agreement). NIH states that HeLa was derived from biopsy specimens of Henrietta Lacks, a 31-year-old patient treated for cervical cancer at Johns Hopkins in 1951, and that the specimens were obtained without her knowledge or consent. It also describes the 2013 NIH-Lacks Family Agreement, controlled access for HeLa genome data, and the working group's role in considering access requests. This supports the central account and Figure 88.5.
- National Institutes of Health, [The HeLa Genome: An Agreement on Privacy and Access](https://stagetestdomain3.nih.gov/about-nih/who-we-are/nih-director/statements/hela-genome-agreement-privacy-access) and [Significant Research Advances Enabled by HeLa Cells](https://osp.od.nih.gov/hela-cells/significant-research-advances-enabled-by-hela-cells/). These NIH histories explain that HeLa was the first human cell line to be stably cultured for research, describe its broad biomedical role, and explain why public genome data raised privacy implications for known descendants. They inform the chapter's account of utility and Figure 88.1, which is an original conceptual timeline.
- Johns Hopkins Medicine, [Henrietta Lacks frequently asked questions](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/henrietta-lacks/frequently-asked-questions). The institution identifies Lacks as an African American mother of five, records that extra tissue was collected for research without her consent under common 1950s practices, and says its cervical-cancer tissue practice applied regardless of race or socioeconomic status. It also discusses later forums on consent, privacy, and communication with donors. This supports the chapter's historical context without treating that context as a moral excuse or asserting a race-specific tissue protocol.
- Johns Hopkins Medicine, [The Legacy of Henrietta Lacks](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/henrietta-lacks) and [Role of the Johns Hopkins Hospital](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/henrietta-lacks/role-of-the-johns-hopkins-hospital). Johns Hopkins records that it was among the few leading hospitals serving African Americans when Lacks was treated, and later says it could and should have done more to inform and work with the Lacks family. These direct institutional accounts support the chapter's treatment of racialized access, institutional authority, and family exclusion, while preserving the distinction between that setting and a race-specific collection policy.
- U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Office for Human Research Protections, [Coded Private Information or Biospecimens Used in Research](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/coded-private-information-or-biospecimens-used-research.html) and [Revised Common Rule preamble](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/regulations/2018-req-preamble/index.html). These sources describe the modern regulatory framework for coded and identifiable biospecimens, including that broad consent for every nonidentified biospecimen use was not adopted. They ground the chapter's caveat that current consent requirements depend on the material and research context.
- National Institutes of Health, [HeLa Genome Data Use Agreement](https://www.grants.nih.gov/sites/default/files/flmngr/HeLa-Genome-Data-Use-Agreement.pdf). The data-use terms request acknowledgment of Henrietta Lacks and her family, require approved users to follow the agreement, and require annual feedback about research uses. This supports the chapter's distinction between recognition and enforceable stewardship.
- Bookshop.org, [The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks](https://bookshop.org/p/books/the-immortal-life-of-henrietta-lacks-rebecca-skloot/12230379). Used only for the page's outbound purchase link and publication details.
