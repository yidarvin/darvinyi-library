# The Structure of Scientific Revolutions — research notes

Verified 2026-07-28. These sources support the chapter's factual claims and scope. The
chapter's prose and diagrams are original synthesis, not a reproduction of the book, its
figures, or its expression.

- Thomas S. Kuhn, [*The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962)](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/kuhn.htm). This primary text is the source for the chapter's account of normal science, anomaly, crisis, scientific revolution, and the return to a new period of puzzle solving. The chapter organizes the ideas thematically rather than following Kuhn's chapter sequence, and its six diagrams are original compositions.
- Thomas S. Kuhn, [*The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*, 1970 edition with the Postscript](https://njw.fish/static/posts/incommensurability/kuhn-structure.pdf). The later primary edition supports the more careful treatment of paradigm, exemplars, and incommensurability in the caveats section. It also guards against reducing a paradigm to a lone theory or treating incommensurability as an end to comparison.
- Thomas S. Kuhn, ["Objectivity, Value Judgment, and Theory Choice" (1977)](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.7208/9780226217239-014/html?lang=en). Kuhn's later primary essay supports the claim that theory choice uses values such as accuracy, consistency, scope, simplicity, and fruitfulness, without those values becoming a mechanical ranking rule.
- University of Chicago Press, [*The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo13179751.html). The publisher page confirms the book's current edition and provides the chapter's outward purchase link.
