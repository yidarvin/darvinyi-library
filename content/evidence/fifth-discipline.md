# The Fifth Discipline — research notes

Verified 2026-07-26. These author, institutional, and publisher sources inform the chapter's
account of the book and its context. The chapter is original synthesis, and its diagrams are
original conceptual illustrations rather than reproductions of the book or any source figure.

- Peter M. Senge, [faculty profile](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/faculty/directory/peter-m-senge), MIT Sloan. MIT identifies Senge as a Senior Lecturer in Leadership and Sustainability, founding chair of the Society of Organizational Learning, and author of *The Fifth Discipline*. Its description says the work develops aspiration, reflective conversation, and understanding of complexity in pursuit of a learning organization. It informs the chapter's account of systems thinking as the integrating practice.
- Peter Senge and Wanda Orlikowski, [Organizations as Enacted Systems: Learning, Knowing and Change](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-963-organizations-as-enacted-systems-learning-knowing-and-change-fall-2002/), MIT OpenCourseWare, 2002. The course description describes organizations as systems that people continually shape and that then influence their action. It supports the chapter's treatment of recurring structures and feedback as both consequential and changeable.
- Penguin Random House, [The Fifth Discipline](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/163984/the-fifth-discipline-by-peter-m-senge/). The publisher describes the revised edition, its learning-organization framing, and its strategies for questioning constraining assumptions, teamwork, and seeing whole systems. The page is also used for this chapter's outbound reader link.
